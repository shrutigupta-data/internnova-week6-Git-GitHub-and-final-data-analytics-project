# Business Insights and Recommendations

## Evidence-based insights

1. **The overall cohort is above the minimum pass threshold, but not uniformly strong.** The mean student average is 72.22/100; 21 of 24 students (87.5%) pass when every subject must be at least 40. Three students (12.5%) fail under this rule.
2. **Science is the clearest department-level support priority in this sample.** Its six students average 65.79, the lowest department mean, and three of six pass (50%). Engineering averages 78.46 and all six students pass, a 12.67-point average gap versus Science.
3. **English is the lowest-mean subject, while computer science is the highest.** English averages 70.67 and computer science 74.83, a 4.17-point difference. The subject means suggest reviewing English outcomes first, while the gap is not large enough by itself to diagnose a cause.
4. **Attendance and marks move together strongly in this dataset.** The Pearson correlation between attendance and student average marks is 0.966 for 24 records. This is a descriptive association only; it does not establish that attendance causes higher marks, and the data is synthetic and small.
5. **The IQR rule flags no numeric outliers.** No subject mark, attendance value, or student average falls outside its 1.5-IQR fences. This makes the present distributions easier to summarize, but does not rule out data-entry errors that remain inside valid ranges.
6. **The file supports group comparisons, not longitudinal conclusions.** There is no date or term field. The analysis cannot identify improvement/decline over time, seasonal effects, or within-student changes.

## Recommendations

1. **Run a focused diagnostic for the Science department.** Review the three failing students' subject-level marks, identify the topics causing scores below 40, and provide targeted tutoring or practice. Recheck the department pass rate after the next assessment before scaling the intervention.
2. **Review English assessment results and provide topic-level support where item data confirms a gap.** English has the lowest subject mean (70.67); use assessment-level evidence before changing curriculum or allocating resources.
3. **Track attendance and achievement together in future reporting, without treating correlation as proof of cause.** Add term/date and student-level follow-up data, then check whether the relationship persists across larger cohorts and after accounting for other factors.

## Scope note

These are practice recommendations from 24 synthetic records, not claims about a real student population. Department labels may not describe comparable cohorts; consult educators and validate data before acting on decisions that affect students.
